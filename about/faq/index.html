Frequently Asked Questions

Last updated: September 6, 2026

Q. Is Zoidium open source?

Yes. The full source is available on GitHub. The repository contains no Panzoid-derived resources, so everything is fully usable under the MIT license.

Q. How is this possible? Panzoid isn't open source.

I load CM3 with the network tab of my browser's DevTools open, which shows where the client gets the resources it needs. I then run additional scripts on top of those resources to build extension-like features around the app. No reverse engineering, decompilation, or unauthorized access is involved. Everything is done through legitimate means.

Q. Can I load projects I made on Panzoid?

Yes. Aside from a few deliberate bug fixes, you can expect exactly the same rendering results as Panzoid. You can also load .pz files.

Q. Can I use the videos I make commercially?

Your work is yours. That said, the rights to any materials, music, and fonts you load, as well as Panzoid's official terms, need to be checked separately. For details, please read the Terms of Use.

Q. Do you collect my personal information?

No. Zoidium does not collect your personal information. For details, please read the Privacy Policy.

Q. How long did it take to build?

One week.

Q. Did you use AI in development?

Yes. I love building with LLMs (large language models) and know my way around them fairly well. I put everything I have into making the project exactly what I want it to be while keeping the code quality high. One of the reasons I created Zoidium was also to explore the limits of LLMs. Realistically, getting this much done in a single week wouldn't have been possible without them. For the record, no other generative AI was used at all: no images, video, or music.

Q. Which AI tools did you use?

For agent harnesses, I used development tools such as Codex by OpenAI and OpenCode by Anomaly. Alongside those, I also developed with T3 Code by Theo Browne. The models included GPT-6 Astra, GPT-5.6 Sol, GPT-5.6 Luna, and GLM-5.3 Flash.

Q. Doesn't AI-generated content infringe copyright?

That claim doesn't always hold. See the U.S. Copyright Office's page on copyright and artificial intelligence.
